# Doyers Street — Game Bible

Nov 25, 2026 · @Kuba

## Overview

*Doyers Street* is a black-and-white noir point-and-click adventure set
in New York in winter 1930: one missing doctor, one hidden detective,
one conspiracy that runs from a Chinatown opium den to Washington.

**Logline:** A broke, divorced private eye who hides who he loves is
hired to find a missing Army doctor. The trail leads into the one world
Matt can't enter without risking his own secret, and up a chain of
mobsters, drug makers and officials who would kill to keep it quiet.

**Working title:** *Doyers Street* (alt: *The Bloody Angle*, *Permit to
Kill*).

**Genre:** Classic third-person point-and-click adventure: exploration,
dialogue, inventory and deduction puzzles. Hard-boiled voice-over
narration.

### Pillars

1.  **Masks.** Everyone passes as something: Matt as straight, Inéz as a
    society girl with money, a drug as medicine, a cop as the law. Every
    act strips one mask.

2.  **Start from nothing.** The player knows only that a boyfriend is
    missing. The conspiracy is earned clue by clue, never explained up
    front.

3.  **Friends are the only honest thing.** Matt, Pete and Kate are the
    heart. Their bond breaks in Act 3 and is rebuilt in Act 4.

4.  **Real 1930, invented crimes.** Every historical detail is accurate
    and drives a story beat; every character and crime is fictional.

5.  **Choices with a cost.** Honesty opens doors but exposes Matt. The
    ending is decided by what he is willing to lose.

## World: New York, winter 1930

The game runs from the first snow to New Year's Eve, one year into the
Depression. Every real backdrop below feeds a plot beat.

| Backdrop                                     | How it drives the story                                                                                                                         |
|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| One year after the Crash                     | Matt's rent is overdue, Pete's paper is cutting staff, Halcyon only survived by going crooked, Inéz's family is ruined.                         |
| Prohibition (repealed only in 1933)          | Speakeasies are neutral ground where cops, crooks, reporters and the queer crowd all drink. The mob controls the supply.                        |
| Medicinal and industrial alcohol permits     | Alcohol could still be made legally under federal permit. Diverting it to bootleggers was a huge real racket. Halcyon holds such permits.       |
| Veterans Administration (created July 1930)  | A brand-new federal agency handing out big contracts. The Serenol veterans' contract is the government link.                                    |
| Bank of United States collapse (11 Dec 1930) | Real run on a bank full of immigrant savings. Background chaos in Act 3; Natalia loses her savings, raising the stakes of her loyalty in Act 4. |
| The "Pansy Craze"                            | Drag balls and queer clubs in the Village and Harlem are briefly fashionable, but raids are starting. Many clubs are mob-owned.                 |
| Vice squad corruption                        | NYC was then investigating vice cops who framed and shook down people. Corrigan runs exactly that racket.                                       |
| Doyers Street, Chinatown                     | The crooked street nicknamed "the Bloody Angle" after the tong wars. Matt's office sits on it: cheap rent, bad reputation, good hiding place.   |
| WWI veterans                                 | Thousands of wounded, many addicted to morphine. They are the test subjects of the drug at the centre of the plot.                              |

## The conspiracy (team only)

Michael is alive. He found a three-way link between Halcyon, the bootleg
mob and a federal official, and they tried to kill him for it. The
player starts knowing nothing; see the reveal ladder.

Money and product run in a loop: permits flow down from Washington,
alcohol and Serenol flow to the mob, bribes flow back up, and the street
and the veterans pay for it.

### What really happened

1.  **The mob.** Halcyon nearly went bankrupt in the Crash. Whitcombe
    saved it by selling alcohol made under its federal medicinal permits
    to Sal Brancato's bootleggers.

2.  **The government.** Harold Grayson, the official who signed those
    permits, takes Brancato's money. In return he steers a new Veterans
    Administration contract to Halcyon for *Serenol*, a "non-addictive"
    morphine substitute.

3.  **The pharmaceutical.** Serenol is addictive. The veterans' trial
    Michael supervises on Governors Island is rigged to win the
    contract. Surplus Serenol goes to the street through Brancato's
    speakeasies and Chinatown.

4.  **The police.** Lt. Corrigan is the mob's man on the force: he keeps
    the vice squad off Brancato's clubs and blackmails their queer
    regulars into silence.

5.  Michael notices the addiction, then the night trucks, then the
    permit signatures. His notebook links all of it.

6.  A relapsed patient, Walter Gibbs, points Michael to The Velvet Door.
    Michael pawns the notebook inside his medical bag for safekeeping
    and goes to watch a handover. Brancato's men, tipped by Corrigan,
    stab him in the alley.

7.  Julian Moss, the club's pianist and one of Corrigan's blackmail
    victims, sees it and hides Michael in a Harlem rooming house.
    Michael can't go to the police or the Army: he doesn't know how high
    it goes.

8.  Whitcombe pays Inéz (broke since the Crash) to find the notebook,
    calling it "stolen company property". She hires Matt. She loves
    Michael and wants him safe, but she also needs the money.

**The irony:** Corrigan is the same vice cop Matt fled the force to
avoid. To save Michael, Matt has to walk back into the one man's sight
line he's spent years avoiding.

## Reveal ladder

The player starts knowing nothing. Each act climbs one rung of the
conspiracy, and each rung costs Matt something personal.

| Point    | What the player believes                                         | Mask that falls                                            | What it costs Matt                                                |
|----------|------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prologue | A society girl's boyfriend walked out on her. A routine job.     | —                                                          | Nothing yet; he needs the \$50                                    |
| Act 1    | Michael got mixed up in drugs, maybe as a user (red herring).    | The drug: "medicine" is sold in a speakeasy back room      | Corrigan sees him at The Velvet Door                              |
| Act 2    | Halcyon is cooking its books and selling to the bootleg mob.     | The company and the cop: Corrigan is on Brancato's payroll | Kate's job, Natalia's old wounds, trust in Inéz                   |
| Act 3    | Michael is alive, and his notebook shows Washington is in on it. | The government: Grayson signed everything                  | His friendship with Pete, and his secret, now in Corrigan's hands |
| Act 4    | The whole chain, and which gala guests belong to it.             | Matt's own mask, if he chooses                             | Decided by the ending                                             |

**Rule for writers:** no character says "conspiracy", names Grayson, or
links the mob to Halcyon before the rung where the player earns it.
Suspicion is fine; facts wait.

## Characters

Every main character has a public face and a secret; the secrets are the
plot. Michael is straight, and only Matt carries the hidden-sexuality
burden among the leads.

### Matt Long — protagonist

- **Age / job:** 37, private investigator, ex-NYPD detective.

- **Look:** tall, rumpled grey suit, loosened tie, fedora with a dented
  crown, two-day stubble, tired eyes that miss nothing.

- **Voice:** dry, self-mocking, hard-boiled one-liners; softer when no
  one's listening.

- **Public face:** washed-up ex-cop who drinks a bit too much, divorced,
  broke.

- **Secret:** bisexual. Left the force partly because a vice-squad
  colleague, Corrigan, got close to finding out.

- **Wants:** rent money, a case that matters, to be left alone.

- **Needs:** to stop living entirely behind a mask.

- **Arc:** from hiding and surviving to choosing what truth is worth,
  and paying for it.

### Pete Duckerton — best friend

- **Age / job:** ~35, crime reporter at the *New York Evening Clarion*.

- **Look:** boyish face, hat pushed back, press card in the band, scarf
  always untied.

- **Voice:** fast, funny, all enthusiasm and bad ideas.

- **Personality:** Peter Pan syndrome: charming, reckless, never quite
  grew up.

- **Secret:** owes money to a bookie who turns out to be Brancato's man.

- **Relationship to Matt:** straight, knows Matt likes men and has never
  cared. The one person Matt doesn't perform for.

- **Arc:** his hunger for the scoop wrecks everything in Act 3; in Act 4
  he learns to wait until the story is true.

### Kate Duckerton — the sensible one

- **Age / job:** ~33, accountant at Halcyon Laboratories, about to
  become the first woman head of accounts.

- **Look:** neat bob, wire spectacles, tailored coat, always carrying a
  ledger or a newspaper.

- **Voice:** precise, dry, impatient with nonsense, warm underneath.

- **Secret:** has noticed the books don't add up and said nothing
  because of the promotion.

- **Arc:** career vs. conscience. Fired and framed in Act 3; in Act 4
  she produces the duplicate ledgers she quietly kept, the evidence that
  breaks the case.

### Natalia Long (née Kowalska) — the ex-wife

- **Age / job:** ~34, Polish immigrant, works at a Greenpoint bakery.

- **Look:** strong features, flour on her sleeves, Sunday hat for
  church.

- **Voice:** blunt, mixes Polish into English when emotional.

- **Secret:** knows Matt's secret; it was the real, never-spoken reason
  for the divorce.

- **Status:** newly engaged to Stefan, a decent butcher.

- **Role:** knows Inéz's Polish mother from church; translates the
  Polish letters; loses her savings in the bank collapse and still hides
  Matt in Act 4.

- **Arc:** from bitterness to a quiet, adult peace with Matt.

### Inéz Venta — the client

- **Age:** ~28, Upper West Side; Puerto Rican father, Polish mother.

- **Look:** platinum blonde waves, dark eyes, fur collar over a coat
  that's been mended once too often.

- **Voice:** smoky, poised, switches to Spanish or Polish when cornered.

- **Public face:** glamorous society girl, girlfriend of a war-hero
  doctor.

- **Secret:** her family was ruined in the Crash; she's secretly taking
  Whitcombe's money to find Michael's notebook.

- **Arc:** femme fatale who truly loves Michael. Trust her in Act 2 and
  she chooses him over the money; cut her loose and she stays a question
  mark.

### Capt. Dr. Michael Harlan — the missing man

- **Age / job:** 36, Army Medical Corps surgeon on Governors Island,
  decorated in France.

- **Look:** square-jawed, uniform in photos; gaunt and feverish when
  found.

- **Voice:** controlled, clipped, haunted by the war.

- **What he found:** the link between Halcyon, the bootleg mob and a
  federal official. Kept it all in a leather notebook.

- **Status:** alive, stabbed, hiding in Harlem.

- **Role:** off-screen for three acts; seen only through what he left
  behind.

### Julian Moss — the witness

- **Age / job:** ~30, jazz pianist at The Velvet Door.

- **Look:** sharp three-piece suit, long fingers, guarded smile.

- **Voice:** wry, careful, says less than he knows.

- **Secret:** gay; one of Corrigan's blackmail victims. Saw Michael
  stabbed and is hiding him.

- **Role:** won't trust Matt until Matt is honest about himself. Mirrors
  the life Matt hides.

### Lt. Frank Corrigan — the cop

- **Look:** heavyset, red-faced, rosary in his pocket, immaculate
  overcoat.

- **Voice:** friendly, fatherly, always slightly threatening.

- **Public face:** respected, church-going vice-squad lieutenant.

- **Secret:** the mob's man on the force. Keeps raids off Brancato's
  clubs, blackmails their queer regulars, knew Matt on the force.

- **Role:** main on-screen antagonist; the man who can destroy Matt with
  one word.

### Salvatore "Sal" Brancato — the mob

- **Look:** small, elegant, grey spats, never raises his voice.

- **Public face:** owner of Brancato Haulage and several "social clubs",
  including The Velvet Door.

- **Secret:** buys Halcyon's permit alcohol and moves surplus Serenol
  through his speakeasies and Chinatown.

- **Role:** the muscle. His men stabbed Michael. Survives most endings:
  the mob always does.

### Arthur Whitcombe — the company

- **Look:** silver hair, pince-nez, charity pin on his lapel.

- **Public face:** VP of Halcyon, philanthropist, veterans' charity
  patron.

- **Secret:** saved Halcyon in the Crash by selling permit alcohol to
  Brancato; now selling an addictive drug to the government as "safe".

- **Role:** corporate face of the conspiracy; Kate's boss; hires Inéz.

### Harold Grayson — the government

- **Look:** grey, forgettable, the kind of man nobody photographs.

- **Public face:** Washington official on the Veterans Administration
  contracts board, formerly in charge of NY alcohol permits.

- **Secret:** signed Halcyon's permits and steered it the Serenol
  contract, paid by Brancato.

- **Role:** the hidden top of the chain. Never named before Act 3; first
  seen at the Plaza gala.

### Lee Wing — the landlord

- **Look:** older man, cardigan, abacus behind the counter.

- **Public face:** owns the tea house under Matt's office.

- **Secret:** tong-connected; knows every crate that moves through
  Chinatown.

- **Role:** ambiguous ally; rent extensions for favours; resents the mob
  muscling into Chinatown.

## Choices and endings

Two tracked choices shape the finale; the last choice at the Plaza

## Gameplay systems and UI

Classic point-and-click at the core, with two noir-specific systems: the
evidence board and the Mask.

| System              | How it works                                                                                                                                            |
|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Interaction         | One-click smart cursor: look, use, talk, walk. Right-click = Matt's voice-over description of anything.                                                 |
| Inventory           | Fedora-shaped pop-up at the top. Items combine by drag (e.g. press card + greatcoat = disguise).                                                        |
| Evidence board      | In Matt's office. Pin clues as photos and notes, connect them with red string. Correct links unlock deductions that open new leads or dialogue options. |
| Matt's notebook     | Journal UI: case notes in his handwriting, people met, open leads. Doubles as the hint system.                                                          |
| The Mask            | Key dialogues offer a *guarded* and an *honest* option, marked with a small half-mask icon. Silently tracked; feeds scenes 3.1, 3.2 and the epilogue.   |
| Character switching | Act 4 only: Matt (locks, fists), Pete (talks his way anywhere, press card), Kate (reads numbers, forges paperwork). Portrait buttons switch.            |
| Map                 | Subway-map-style city map; locations unlock as leads appear. Travel by cab, ferry or El train.                                                          |
| Time pressure       | Two timed scenes only: Halcyon night watchman (2.2) and the pawnshop (4.2). No fail state elsewhere.                                                    |
| Saves               | Auto-save per scene + manual slots.                                                                                                                     |
| Accessibility       | Hotspot highlight key, adjustable text speed, subtitles always available, no timed puzzle without an easy mode.                                         |

## Art direction

Pure black and white, lit like a 1940s film noir: hard shadows do the
storytelling.

- **Palette:** greyscale only, 8–12 tones per scene, deep blacks,
  blown-out highlights from lamps and neon.

- **Lighting:** venetian-blind stripes, single bulbs, car headlights,
  rain on windows, cigarette glow. Every scene has one dominant light
  source.

- **Camera:** fixed hand-painted backgrounds with Dutch angles for
  tension (Velvet Door alley, precinct). Close-up cutaway frames for key
  dialogue.

- **Characters:** stylised, slightly elongated proportions; silhouettes
  readable at a glance (Matt's fedora, Inéz's fur collar, Corrigan's
  bulk, Brancato's spats).

- **Texture:** film grain, subtle gate weave, vignette. Optional
  CRT/projector filter in settings.

- **Typography:** period sans for UI (Futura-like); Matt's notebook in
  handwritten script; newspaper headlines in condensed slab type.

- **Reference touchstones:** *The Maltese Falcon*, *Double Indemnity*,
  *Grim Fandango*'s noir framing, Sin City's high contrast without the
  colour accents.

- **Period accuracy:** 1930 cars, the El trains, Chinatown signage,
  speakeasy peepholes, Army uniforms of the period. No art deco
  anachronisms past 1930.

An accent colour (e.g. Inéz's red lipstick) is still an open question,
see the last section.

## Sound and music

Original score in a 1930 jazz idiom, with a lonely muted trumpet as
Matt's theme.

| Cue                | Where                   | Sound                                                              |

| Matt's theme       | Office, map, voice-over | Muted trumpet, brushed snare, upright bass                         |
| Doyers Street      | Chinatown hub           | Street ambience, distant erhu, mahjong tiles, rain                 |
| The Velvet Door    | Speakeasy               | Stride piano (Julian, played in-scene), laughter, clinking glasses |
| Harlem             | Club and rooming house  | Hot jazz band, then a single piano through the wall                |
| Greenpoint         | Bakery and church       | Polish hymn, accordion, ovens                                      |
| Halcyon / precinct | Tension scenes          | Ticking clocks, low strings, typewriters                           |
| The Plaza          | Finale                  | Society dance orchestra that drops out as the truth comes out      |
| Epilogue           | Doyers Street           | Matt's theme on solo piano, snow ambience                          |

**Voice:** full voice acting if budget allows; Matt's narration is
essential either way. Light period accents (Polish, Puerto Rican
Spanish, Irish New York, Italian New York, Cantonese for Lee Wing's
lines to staff), never caricature.

**Diegetic radio:** Matt's office radio plays period-style news
bulletins that track the real backdrop (bank collapse, Prohibition
raids) and, later, the fallout of Pete's story.

## Writing and voice guide

Hard-boiled on the surface, human underneath: every joke covers a wound.

### Rules

- Matt's voice-over is short, wry, visual. One image per line, no
  speeches.

- Nobody explains the conspiracy before its rung on the reveal ladder.

- Matt's sexuality is never a punchline. Hostile characters use period
  slurs sparingly and only to show who they are; sympathetic queer
  characters use their own words ("in the life", "one of us").

- Every main character speaks differently: Pete fast and funny, Kate
  exact, Natalia blunt with Polish, Inéz poised with Spanish or Polish
  when cornered, Corrigan fatherly and menacing.

- Period slang yes (dough, gumshoe, flatfoot, bulls, giggle water, the
  bank), but never so thick it needs a glossary.

### Sample lines

**Matt, examining the eviction notice:** "Love letter from the landlord.
He always writes when the money's late."

**Matt, when Inéz walks in:** "She came in out of the rain like she'd
paid it to stop."

**Pete, morning after:** "Coffee's hot. Kate made it, so don't
thank me."

**Kate:** "Pete, a story isn't true because it's exciting. It's true
because it adds up."

**Natalia (2.3):** "You never lied to me, Mateusz. You just never told
me anything. That's worse."

**Corrigan (1.6):** "Long. Didn't figure this for your kind of place."

**Julian (3.2):** "Every cop who comes in here wants something. Tell me
what you want, and tell me the truth, or play your own piano."

**Matt, honest choice (3.2):** "I'm not here as a cop. I'm here because
I know what it costs to walk in that door."

**Matt, epilogue, Headline ending:** "They took my licence and my good
name. Turns out neither one ever fit."
